KNOW WHAT TO ASK

Billing Guide

Understand common billing terms and ask clear, useful questions.

Billing basics

Start with the language on your statement.

What is a billing code?

A billing code identifies a service, procedure, supply, or diagnosis for billing and reporting. Code systems vary by country and payer. Ask the provider which code set and year applies to your statement.

What is a duplicate charge?

Two similar entries can sometimes be valid—for example, separate dates, units, or providers. Ask whether the entries represent distinct services and request the dates, quantities, and documentation supporting each one.

What is an explanation of benefits (EOB)?

An EOB is typically a payer's explanation of how a claim was processed. It is generally not itself a bill. Compare its allowed amount, adjustments, and patient responsibility with the provider statement.

What does “out of network” mean?

It generally refers to a provider or facility that does not have a contract with a health plan. The financial impact depends on your plan, local rules, and the circumstances of care.

What should I do if a code looks unfamiliar?

Request the plain-language description, service date, units, ordering clinician, and documentation supporting the charge. An unfamiliar code alone does not prove an error.

What is an itemized bill?

A statement listing individual services, supplies, quantities, codes, and charges. It is often more useful for reviewing than a single total balance.

CONVERSATION STARTER

Questions worth asking

  • Can you provide an itemized statement?
  • What service does this code represent?
  • Why does this item appear more than once?
  • What date and quantity does this charge cover?
  • Can you explain the adjustment and patient balance?
  • Can the account be placed on hold while this is reviewed?

WE'RE HERE TO HELP

Help & Support

A practical guide to reviewing a bill and following up.

01

Before you start

Gather the itemized bill, any explanation of benefits, receipts, and relevant dates. Keep a copy of everything you send.

02

When you find a concern

Record the code, line-item description, amount, and why it needs clarification. Ask neutral questions before alleging an error.

03

When contacting a provider

Use the provider's official billing contact. Ask for a reference number and whether the disputed balance can be paused during review.

04

Protect your information

Only share necessary information through verified, secure channels. Redact unrelated personal details from documents where appropriate.

Important limitation

OctoHealth is a frontend demonstration. It uses transparent, basic checks on entered line items; it does not perform real OCR, connect to official billing-code databases, submit insurance claims, or provide legal advice.
